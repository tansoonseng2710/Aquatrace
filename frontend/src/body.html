Lab results

Add laboratory results from a file or by hand. Each sample is paired with the sensor readings taken nearest to it in time and place, and those pairs train the model.

Upload CSV

Columns: sample_id, timestamp, lat, lon, compound, concentration, unit, lod, method, notes. Units ng/L, µg/L or mg/L are converted. Values below LOD may be written as <LOD; they are stored as LOD/2 and flagged. Sensor columns (ph, orp, turbidity, uv, temp, conductivity, do) are optional: if present they are used directly instead of matching.

For every lab result, the Colab model screens only the sensor readings taken with that sample, without seeing the lab value. Then the two are compared. Which algorithm? 7 compared

US screening model · testing set = rivers never seen
AlgorithmCross-validation mAP (we choose by this)CV mAPTest accuracyPrecisionRecallF1Test mAP
Why not pick the highest accuracy? Most water is low, so a dummy that never flags anything scores the highest accuracy and finds no polluted sample. mAP measures what matters: are the truly high samples at the top of the list. Logistic regression, SVM and naive Bayes answer yes/no, so they are compared here for screening; the in-browser trainer above estimates a level (a number) and offers the level versions: random forest, gradient boosting, ridge regression, k-nearest neighbours and a decision tree.

Interview notes

How it works

Sensors run continuously. Lab samples arrive in batches and check the model. The two meet in a paired dataset.

Scientific positioning

HPLCAnalytical reference. Identifies and quantifies specific compounds. Never replaced.
SensorsContinuous, real-time bulk measurements. Cannot identify individual micropollutants.
Machine learningRelationship modelling and estimation. Output is a screening indication.
GPSSpatial investigation: where abnormal readings occur and repeat.
Photocatalytic reactorExperimental treatment evaluation. Effectiveness is decided by the data.
Core question. Can continuously measured water-quality parameters, combined with machine learning and validated against HPLC, give a useful real-time indication of pharmaceutical micropollutant contamination? Secondary. Can the same data framework evaluate photocatalytic treatment and reveal spatial patterns that help investigate pollution sources?
